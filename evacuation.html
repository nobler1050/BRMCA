Ready, Set, Flee: Your Wildfire Evacuation Plan

Introduction

The Blue Ridge Mountain Civic Association (BRMCA) offers this plan to residents living along both sides of Blue Ridge Mountain and Raven Rocks Roads. It was developed in consultation with officials from Loudoun and Clarke Counties as well as businesses and employers.

While Clarke County and the Mountain Overlay district of Loudoun County are rated as having a moderate risk of wildfire on a national scale, within the state of Virginia our ridge is in the 90th percentile for wildfire risk. A May 2025 assessment by the Virginia Department of Forestry rated the BRMCA community area as having a high risk (143 rating). That risk goes up considerably in periods of drought and the presence of additional visitors.

This plan presents steps every home dweller in our area needs to consider in preparing for the possibility of a wildfire occurring on the ridge. It has two main sections: pre-fire preparation and planning, and evacuation planning.

Be Prepared

  1. Sign up for Crisis24 (previously called CodeRed)
    Crisis24, provided by Onsolve, is the warning application used by Loudoun and Clarke Counties to alert areas of emergencies. Download the Crisis24 app, or update your current CodeRed app, then follow the instructions for selecting the types of alerts you wish to receive.
  2. Harden your home
    While it’s impossible to fireproof a forest, you can significantly reduce wildfire risk by creating defensible space around your home. The website NFPA — Preparing Homes for Wildfire has in-depth information on steps you can take to reduce the chances of your home being burned. (Note that the zones specified on this website should be expanded on the downslope side of your home up to 300 feet.) The more of these suggestions you implement the more you reduce the chances of your home catching fire.
  3. If you have to leave, have a plan for what to take
    Don’t wait until you’re in the middle of an emergency to start thinking about what to pack. Here are two links with recommendations and considerations for your “go bag”:
    • Ready for Wildfire — Emergency Supply Kit
    • Project Wildfire — Go Kit

    The link below provides information about what documents, data, and valuables you should plan to take, as well as tips on having the proper insurance and insurance documentation in the event your home is destroyed:

    • FEMA — Safeguard Critical Documents and Valuables (PDF)

    Here are two comprehensive sites that focus on overall emergency preparedness and financial preparedness. They are useful for broad planning for life’s contingencies, as well as specific information on wildfire preparedness and planning.

    • Virginia Emergency Management — Emergency Kit
    • Ready.gov — Financial Preparedness

    Part of financial preparedness is creating a home inventory for insurance purposes. It helps you know if you are adequately insured, and it gives you documentation to support a claim for loss in the event you lose items in a fire. The link below is an example tutorial from Progressive Insurance outlining what information should be included.

    • Progressive — How to Create a Home Inventory

    The above preparation cannot wait until an emergency hits. It is critical to make these preparations early and to review your plans annually.

  4. Make a communications plan to account for all family members
    Some family members might not be home should you evacuate. If an evacuation occurs it’s critical that all family members know how to get in touch. This site is an excellent guide for making a communications plan:
    • Ready.gov — Family Emergency Communication Plan (PDF)
  5. Know your neighbors — some may need assistance
    Create a voluntary list of vulnerable neighbors (e.g., elderly or disabled). Perhaps designate two members of your defined “neighborhood” to maintain a call tree to ensure everyone knows the situation. Pay special attention to anyone who may need assistance in evacuating. Keep in mind they might also need assistance in hardening their homes.

During a Fire

  1. Read the Crisis24 instructions carefully.
  2. Recommended evacuation routes are going to be predicated on the location of the fire. Pay close attention.
  3. The Crisis24 instructions may tell you where emergency shelters are if needed.
  4. Notify disadvantaged neighbors, if any. Check to see if they need/want a ride.
  5. Execute your family communication plan.
  6. Gather your go bag contents.
  7. Secure your home. Here is an excellent set of instructions from the Cal Fire website for guidance.
  8. Leave early. Don’t take a chance that your evacuation route gets blocked.

Evacuation Process

  1. Take a chain saw if you have one. If trees are down you may be the only one who is able to get to them and clear them.
  2. Loudoun and Clarke fire departments and police will facilitate getting people off the mountain. Their first priority is the safe evacuation of people and pets.
  3. Seek safety if trapped. From Cal Fire, again: Cal Fire guide (p. 25).

After Evacuation

  1. Rely on Crisis24 information to know where to go.
  2. Notify local emergency operations centers and the Red Cross with your names, location, and contact information.
  3. Do not attempt to return until local authorities have indicated it is safe to do so.
