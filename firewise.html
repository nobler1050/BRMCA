Ready, Set, Flee: Your Wildfire Evacuation Plan

1. Introduction

The Blue Ridge Mountain Civic Association (BRMCA) offers this plan to residents living along both sides of Blue Ridge Mountain and Raven Rocks Roads. It was developed in consultation with officials from Loudoun and Clarke Counties as well as businesses/employers.

While Clarke County and the Mountain Overlay district of Loudoun County are rated as having a moderate risk of wildfire on a national scale, within the state of Virginia our ridge is in the 90th percentile for wildfire risk. A May 2025 assessment by the Virginia Department of Forestry rated the BRMCA community area as having a high risk (143 rating). That risk goes up considerably in periods of drought and the presence of additional visitors.

This plan presents steps every home dweller in our area needs to consider in preparing for the possibility of a wildfire occurring on the ridge. It has two main sections: pre-fire preparation and planning, and evacuation planning.

2. Be Prepared

a. Sign up for Crisis24 (previously CodeRed)
Crisis24 is the warning application used by Loudoun and Clarke Counties to alert areas of emergencies. Some of you may have signed up through a weather warning app but may not have opted for other types of emergency warnings. On your smartphone, download the Crisis24 app or update your current CodeRed app (Onsolve is the provider). Follow the instructions for selecting which types of alerts you wish to receive.

b. Harden your home
While it's not possible to fireproof a forest, you can significantly reduce wildfire risk by creating defensible space around your home. The NFPA website has in-depth information on steps you can take to reduce the chances of your home being burned. Keep in mind that the zones specified on this website need to be expanded on the downslope side of your home up to 300 feet. The more of these suggestions you implement, the more you reduce the chances of your home catching fire.

c. Have a plan for what to take
A Go Bag is an emergency evacuation kit containing items you may need to sustain you and your family short term in the event you must leave. The following resources address Go Bag preparation, documents and valuables to take, and financial preparedness:

  • Ready for Wildfire — Emergency Supply Kit
  • Project Wildfire — Go Kit (Oregon-based; local references don't apply, but includes useful guidance on pets)
  • FEMA — Safeguard Critical Documents and Valuables (PDF)
  • Virginia Emergency Management — Emergency Kit
  • Ready.gov — Financial Preparedness

Part of financial preparedness is creating a home inventory for insurance purposes — it helps confirm you are adequately insured and gives you documentation to support a claim in the event of a fire. This Progressive Insurance guide is a useful starting point.

If you do not store your data on the cloud, consider adding an external drive to your system with automatic backups that can be easily taken with you if you must evacuate.

Doing the above preparation cannot wait until an emergency hits. It is critical to make these preparations early and put a tickler on your calendar to review your plans annually.

d. Make a communications plan
It's possible some family members may not be home should you need to evacuate. If an evacuation occurs, it's critical that all family members know how to get in touch. This Ready.gov family emergency communications guide (PDF) is an excellent resource for making a plan.

e. Know your neighbors
Create a voluntary list of vulnerable neighbors (e.g., elderly or disabled). Consider designating two members of your neighborhood to maintain a call tree to ensure everyone knows the situation. Pay special attention to anyone who may need assistance evacuating — and keep in mind these people may also need help hardening their homes.

3. During a Fire
  • Read the Crisis24 instructions carefully. Evacuation routes will depend on the location of the fire — pay close attention.
  • Crisis24 instructions may tell you where emergency shelters are if needed.
  • Notify disadvantaged neighbors and offer assistance.
  • Execute your family communication plan.
  • Gather your Go Bag contents.
  • Secure your home. The Cal Fire Ready, Set, Go guide (p. 23) provides excellent step-by-step instructions.
  • Leave early — don't take a chance that your roads out get blocked.
4. Evacuation Process
  • Take a chain saw if you have one — if trees are down you may be the only one able to clear them.
  • Loudoun and Clarke fire departments and police will facilitate getting people off the mountain. Their first priority is the safe evacuation of people and pets.
  • If trapped, seek safety. The Cal Fire guide (p. 25) has guidance on what to do if you cannot evacuate.
5. After Evacuation
  • Rely on Crisis24 information to know where to go and when it is safe to return.
  • Notify local emergency operations centers and the Red Cross with your names, location, and contact information.
  • Do not attempt to return until local authorities have indicated it is safe to do so.
